# 3. Find your H10 sensor

## Lab situation

There are multiple Polar H10 sensors already in the room. You need to identify **your** sensor and connect to it from the Raspberry Pi.

## Step 1: Find the ID printed on the side of the H10

Look at the side/back of the sensor unit and note the printed identifier. 

![Troubleshooting Polar H9 / H10 heart rate sensor | Polar Global](./img/281698ddd7e2f1b20bd1065f19e095a63b349863-300x116-default.webp)

In many cases the BLE advertising name contains this identifier, for example:

```text
Polar H10 7A4F2C31
```

Your exact value will be different.

## Step 2: Start scanning from the command line

Open a terminal and run:

```bash
bluetoothctl
```

Inside `bluetoothctl`, enter:

```text
power on
agent on
default-agent
scan on
```

You should begin to see nearby Bluetooth devices.

## Step 3: Find your own H10

Watch the scan output and look for a name like:

```text
[NEW] Device AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF Polar H10 7A4F2C31
```

Match the visible name to the ID printed on your physical sensor.

Record:

- the BLE address, for example `AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF`
- the device name, for example `Polar H10 7A4F2C31`

Stop the scan when you have found your device:

```text
scan off
```

## Step 4: Connect and inspect details

Still inside `bluetoothctl`, run:

```text
info AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF
```

Replace the address with your own device address.

The `info` command may show useful details such as:

- whether the device is connected
- RSSI
- UUIDs for advertised services
- manufacturer or appearance data

## What to look for

Try to identify whether you can find evidence of:

- the **Heart Rate Service** (`180D`)
- the **Battery Service** (`180F`)
- the **Heart Rate Measurement characteristic** (`2A37`)
- the **Battery Level characteristic** (`2A19`)

## About power and battery

You may be able to inspect battery-related data through the Battery Service. Power consumption itself is not usually presented directly as a simple BLE characteristic for this device, but battery level is commonly available.

## Task

Create a short record in your notes containing:

- your H10 device name
- its BLE address
- one observed RSSI value
- one service UUID you found
- whether you found Heart Rate and Battery power. 

**NOTE: Exit out of bluetoothctl and/or close the terminal window/command line before moving to the next step**
